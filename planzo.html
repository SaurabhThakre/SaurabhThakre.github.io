The problem

Group trips often stall because dates, budgets and preferences are spread across messages. A popular plan can still be unaffordable or impossible for someone. Asking people to share every private limit in a group chat creates another source of friction.

Planzo explores a different workflow: collect individual constraints privately, use them to inform a shared plan, then make agreement explicit.

Product focus

The project connects trip setup, individual preferences, itinerary review, shared decisions and a final journey view. The portfolio focuses on privacy, feasibility and how the product represents agreement.

A separate fictional Goa sample lets visitors explore the decisions without creating an account. The shared experience supports accounts, invitations and saved trip workspaces.

Three decisions that shaped it

1. Use private inputs to improve the shared decision.

Availability, budget and preferences feed planning without exposing raw private briefs to the group. The interface needs to communicate feasibility while protecting the reasons behind an individual’s constraints.

2. Attach agreement to a plan version.

Members respond to a specific itinerary. Material changes require fresh responses, and silence remains pending. This gives a change in dates, price or headcount a clear consequence for the group’s decision.

3. Keep human review in assisted planning.

Generated itineraries and cost estimates need review. Booking guidance and self-reported booking status do not constitute a reservation. The product keeps planning, agreement and booking distinct.

A short walkthrough

  1. Open the fictional Goa sample and choose Explore the Goa sample.
  2. Review the itinerary, a member’s cost and availability, and the current group responses.
  3. Use Scenarios to explore changes in price, dates, headcount or responses.
  4. Observe whether agreement still holds for the current plan.

The sample saves fictional data in the browser. The default shared app has a separate sign-in and email-verification flow.

Evidence and current limits

Project documentation describes the shared planning flow, a separate sample and implementation checks. These demonstrate prototype progress; they do not establish customer adoption or product-market fit.

The latest project documentation records unreliable complete-route AI generation across live requests. Prices, schedules and locations are estimates to review. Travel-provider booking, payment and reservation confirmation are outside scope.

The question to validate: can a group reach a feasible decision while each person still understands and controls what they are agreeing to?

What I would test next

  • Can participants understand which details stay private?
  • Do they notice when a material change invalidates earlier agreement?
  • Can an organizer resolve an infeasible plan without pressuring someone to reveal private constraints?

Proposed measures include time to reach a feasible plan, unresolved constraints and comprehension of the current agreement state. User-study results are not claimed here.
