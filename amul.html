The problem and discovery

Dispatch planning needs to reconcile demand, available inventory, truck capacity and timing. Under-filled trucks and missed dispatch windows were the focus of my product management internship at Amul (GCMMF), Anand, in April–May 2026.

I visited plants and warehouses and interviewed dispatch and sales stakeholders to understand operational bottlenecks before defining the product scope.

3+plant & warehouse visits
15+stakeholder interviews
10+demand scenarios tested

My contribution

I translated discovery into a PRD and a phased four-module roadmap for an AI-assisted truck-load planning engine. The scope covered demand forecasting, inventory allocation, dispatch planning and load optimization.

I designed shortage-allocation and projected-inventory logic, built a Python/Streamlit prototype and used demand scenarios to test planning behavior. I also presented five AI feature concepts, priorities and success measures to senior leaders.

How the work was structured

Product areaDecision it supports
Demand forecastingUnderstand the quantity and timing of requirements.
Inventory allocationAccount for available stock and shortages.

Load optimizationEvaluate how truck capacity is used.

The public simulator exposes inventory, lead-time, loading-window and trigger-threshold parameters alongside dispatch simulation and truck-capacity exploration. It provides a concrete artifact for discussing how planning assumptions affect a decision.

Validation and outcomes

I simulated three or more modules across more than ten demand scenarios. The work produced a PRD, a phased roadmap and a working prototype, and secured leadership buy-in to advance the concept.

Targets used for evaluation: 90%+ truck fill and one-day dispatch latency. These are product targets; this case study does not claim production achievement of either.

The demonstrated outcome is the progression from operational discovery to a testable planning concept. Production rollout, sustained adoption and realized savings are not established by the available evidence.

Product perspective

The central challenge is balancing truck utilization with timely dispatch under inventory constraints. A useful planning tool needs to expose those constraints and make the effect of assumptions understandable to a planner.

For a next pilot, I would compare recommendations with existing planner decisions across representative demand and shortage scenarios, recording overrides, fill levels, dispatch timing and reasons for disagreement.
