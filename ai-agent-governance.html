The problem

An inventory of agent names is only a starting point. Governance teams need to understand what each agent does, who is accountable, what it can access and whether a requested control actually took effect.

This independent academic case study explores how familiar data-catalog and stewardship concepts can support AI agent governance. It originated in an IIM Bangalore class activity and uses representative enterprise data.

Product hypothesis: connecting discovery, accountability and verified action outcomes will make an agent catalog more useful in an operator’s daily workflow.

My contribution

I designed and prototyped the console, focusing on product framing, catalog information architecture, accountable ownership, risk-review workflows and recovery from partial failures. Deliverables include an interactive prototype, annotated screens and documented product decisions.

AI-assisted prototyping supported the build. The work presented here centers on product scope, interaction design and the meaning of the states an operator sees.

Three decisions that shaped it

1. Start with an operator’s next decision.

The core journey is find → assess → establish accountability → act → verify. Search, High risk, Ownerless and Unused views help users decide where to focus. Agent creation and orchestration sit outside the prototype’s scope.

2. Separate an owner field from acknowledged responsibility.

The agent profile pairs business purpose with ownership and operating context. An ownership acknowledgement makes the distinction between a populated record and accepted accountability visible.

3. Treat partial completion as a first-class result.

A common interface still needs to respect different source-platform capabilities. Before a bulk action, the prototype previews eligibility. Afterwards, it separates confirmed, failed and skipped outcomes and supports retry.

Evidence and current limits

The prototype demonstrates catalog search, risk filtering, agent investigation, ownership journeys, bulk-action eligibility and mixed results. Sample platform names represent inventory sources; their connectors and live control APIs are outside the implementation.

Customer interviews, usability results and measured business outcomes are not yet available. This is a high-fidelity product exploration.

For validation, I would ask governance operators to find a critical agent, identify its accountable owner, investigate permissions and interpret a mixed bulk-action result. I would observe task completion, interpretation errors and recovery decisions.

What I would test next

  • Can an operator distinguish a control request from a confirmed source outcome?
  • Does ownership acknowledgement help teams identify accountability gaps?
  • Which context is essential before acting, and which metadata adds noise?

Proposed measures include time to assess an agent, ownership acknowledgement coverage and unresolved partial failures. These are future evaluation measures.
